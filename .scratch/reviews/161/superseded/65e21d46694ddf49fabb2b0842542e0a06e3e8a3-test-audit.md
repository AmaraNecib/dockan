## 1. `apps/api/src/lib/supplier-ledger.test.ts` (pure math)

Correct:
- `supplierEntrySign` asserts all four kinds incl. that REFUND is a charge
  (+) and RETURN a credit (−) — matches `supplier-ledger.ts:16-18`.
- `supplierBalance` covers sign mix, negative (credit) balance, empty.
- `splitAgainstRemaining` covers fit / excess / nothing-owed (0 remaining).
- `deriveInvoicePaid` (test :60-79) is the strongest test here: it proves a
  PAYMENT naming another invoice is excluded, a `null`-invoice credit payment
  is excluded, and a RETURN naming the invoice is *not* counted as paid —
  exactly the reconciliation contract in the source comment
  (`supplier-ledger.ts:76-85`). Plus a corrupt-over-payment cap and the empty
  case.
- `allocateOldestFirst` covers oldest-first fill, per-invoice cap + undistributed
  tail, settled-invoice skip, deterministic same-date tie-break
  (createdAt → numeric invoice number), and non-positive/NaN → nothing.
- `availableSupplierCredit` covers credit, owed, zero.
- `supplierReturnSplit` covers partial reduction + excess, no invoice (all
  credit), and settled invoice (all credit).

Notes (gaps, low severity):
- `allocateOldestFirst > allocates nothing for a non-positive or non-finite
  amount` (:118-122) only exercises `0` and `NaN`. The name promises
  "non-positive", so a negative amount and `Infinity` are untested; both are
  handled by `Number.isFinite(amount) && amount > 0` (`supplier-ledger.ts:69`).
- The `Math.floor(amount)` in `allocateOldestFirst` is never exercised (all
  fixtures are whole). Fractional input is a real, if unlikely, path since
  amounts arrive from the wire; a fractional case would pin the floor.
- `supplierReturnSplit` never feeds a negative `returnValue`, and
  `invoiceSequence` (`supplier-ledger.ts:45-48`) tie-break with a *non-numeric*
  invoice number ("sorts as 0") is not tested — only numeric INV-#### numbers.

## 2. `apps/api/src/store/supplier-account-store.test.ts` (store seam)

Correct:
- "backfills a charge and a payment entry" (:58-78) is the highest-value store
  test: it seeds a PURCHASE + PAYMENT, deletes the
  `migration.supplierAccountV1` marker to force a real upgrade run, asserts the
  backfill reads the invoice `paid_amount` *cache* (not the payment rows),
  asserts `balance === 700`, and then proves a second `backfillSupplierAccounts`
  call is a marker-gated no-op (no double charge). This directly covers the
  "backfill must not double-charge" risk in `161-design.md`.
- "records a payment entry and keeps the outstanding invoice read in sync"
  (:80-99) ties `insertSupplierEntry` + `applyPaymentToInvoice` to
  `listOutstandingPurchaseInvoices` and asserts remaining drops to 300.
- "stores a supplier return and reads back the per-line returned quantity"
  (:101-139) round-trips `insertSupplierReturn` / `insertSupplierReturnLine` /
  `returnedByPurchaseLine` / `listSupplierReturns` / `getSupplierReturn`.
- "keeps the invoice cache reconcilable" (:141-172, added by `bce8a31`) is the
  reconciliation proof the task asked about: it writes PAYMENT 300 + RETURN 200
  naming the invoice, asserts `detail.paidAmount === deriveInvoicePaid(entries,
  id, total)`, `detail.returnCreditAmount === 200`, and
  `remainingAmount === total − paid − returnCredit`. This is precisely the
  printed-header identity from the design doc and the header it guards.
- "includes the signed account on a supplier party detail" (:174-186) checks a
  negative balance (`-50`) and that a customer's `partyDetail.account` is
  `null` — the "customers keep invoice-based debt" guarantee.

Notes (gaps, low severity):
- No store test for the supplier-delete guard
  (`drizzle-store.ts:1386-1391`: a supplier with entries/returns/invoices is not
  deletable). The new account makes that guard load-bearing; it is currently
  uncovered at this seam.
- `applyDebtOffsetToInvoice` returning **false** (the refusal path the router
  throws `RETURN_DEBT_OFFSET_REFUSED` on, `router.ts:840`) is never exercised;
  only the success (`true`) branch is asserted.
- `allocateSupplierReturnNumber` (PRT-%04d sequence) has no store test.

## 3. `tests/e2e/supplier-account.spec.ts` (Playwright)

Correct — this spec now covers every capability named in the task:
- Oldest-first payment (step 4) → balance 1500.
- FREE-STANDING return (step 5) → balance 1200, invoice untouched.
- INVOICE-linked return (step 6) → balance 200; the header stays consistent.
- Named-invoice payment, `target = INVOICE` (step 7) → balance 0.
- The reconciliation block after step 7 asserts it explicitly:
  `invoice-remaining = 300 دج` and `invoice-returns-credit = 1000 دج`, which is
  the `2000 − 500 (pay) − 1000 (invoice-linked return) − 200 (named pay) = 300`
  identity and the `returnCreditAmount` surface. The comment correctly explains
  the free-standing 300 is credit (account-only) and does not touch the invoice.
- Credit advance, `target = CREDIT` (step 8) → balance container switches to
  «رصيد لنا عند المورّد» and value 500; the negative-credit semantics are
  asserted, not just the number.
- Account refund closing the credit (step 9) → balance 0.

Notes (gaps, low severity):
- The spec asserts the happy path only. It does not cover the guard rails the
  router enforces via `ORPCError`: refund/`cashBack` above the available credit
  (`router.ts:865`, `:850`), a payment above an invoice's remaining
  (`PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_REMAINING`), or an invoice-linked return above the
  returnable quantity per line (`RETURN_EXCEEDS_RETURNABLE`). Those money
  boundaries are the design's stated top risk; they are unit-capped by
  `supplierReturnSplit`, but the *rejection* is not proven end-to-end.
- No e2e coverage for `suppliersDelete` on an account-bearing supplier.
